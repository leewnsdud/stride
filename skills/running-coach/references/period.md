# Period review

Use inclusive local dates. Aggregate all selected activities, identify missing detail/RPE, and distinguish completed linked sessions from unlinked runs and unfulfilled planned sessions. Unlinked does not mean unplanned; no recording does not prove rest. Future planned sessions are upcoming, not missed. Never double-count a linked activity in both totals.

Compare volume/time/ascent/run-days with the immediately preceding equal-length period when supplied; if the current range is unfinished, explain that before comparing. A prior empty period is unknown history, not zero fitness or infinite improvement. Weekly totals are descriptive; do not prescribe workload solely from an acute:chronic ratio or a fixed percent change. Separate road/trail and intended easy/quality/race sessions. Use zone-time only with recording coverage and consistent configured zones; do not label all Z3 as bad or enforce an 80/20 distribution from incomplete watch zones.

Look for practical patterns: key-session spacing, concentration of long/steep efforts, execution of purpose, repeated comparable performance and runner feedback. Session-RPE load is minutes × reported 0–10 RPE in arbitrary units; missing RPE means missing load, not zero. Garmin load is a separate device metric and must not be added to sRPE. More load is not always progress, and less can be a taper/recovery decision. Recommend one priority for the next week and, when needed, ask about recovery or schedule before increasing work. Do not automatically edit the plan.

Evidence:
- [Haddad et al., 2017, session-RPE review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29163016/): practical internal-load monitoring with contextual influences and limitations; RPE recording protocol matters.
- [Bourdon et al., 2017, load-monitoring consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28463642/): combine external/internal load and individual context instead of interpreting one derivative score as a complete readiness assessment.
